## Read this first: Chapter 325N

`mn-guardrails.md` §2 flags Minnesota Statutes chapter 325N. A tax delinquency on an owner-occupied 1–4 unit home makes it a "residence in foreclosure" (Minn. Stat. § 325N.01(f)). Anyone who offers, for compensation, to stop a tax forfeiture, get a payment plan, or help an owner redeem or repurchase is a "solicitor" (§ 325N.01(a)). A solicitor may not then acquire an interest in that home from the owner (§ 325N.04(5)).

Because of that, every draft:

- presents ARCH **only as a buyer**;
- describes payment plans, redemption, repurchase and surplus claims as things the owner does **directly with the county**, and says ARCH does not arrange them;
- never says "you can stay" or "you can buy it back," which would trigger the foreclosure-purchaser regime (§ 325N.10(b)).

The attorney should confirm this framing, and should also review the four live situation pages with 325N in mind. The guardrails file recommends a Minnesota attorney with consumer-protection experience for any 325N question, in addition to ARCH's transaction attorney.
