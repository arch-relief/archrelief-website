# Behind on property taxes in Hennepin County? Here's how tax forfeiture works, and what you can still do.

If property taxes on a Minnesota home go unpaid long enough, the property can forfeit to the State of Minnesota. That takes years, and there are several points along the way where the owner can stop it. This guide walks through each step in Hennepin County, the notices you'll receive, and every option, including the ones that have nothing to do with selling.

*This is general information, not legal advice. For advice about your situation, talk with a licensed Minnesota attorney or call Hennepin County.*

## The timeline, step by step

| Step | What happens | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Taxes are due | Property taxes are due in two halves, May 15 and October 15. Penalties start the day after a due date. | Minn. Stat. § 279.01; Hennepin County |
| 2. Taxes become delinquent | Unpaid taxes become delinquent on the first business day of January after the year they were due. Interest begins. | Minn. Stat. § 279.02 |
| 3. Published list and objection deadline | The county files a delinquent tax list with the district court and publishes it. Owners may pay or file a written objection by the deadline stated in the notice. | DOR Red Book (delinquent tax list notice) |
| 4. Court judgment and "bid in" for the state | Judgment is entered against unpaid parcels. On the second Monday in May, the county auditor bids the parcel in for the state for the amount owed. The owner still owns the property. | Minn. Stat. § 280.01 |
| 5. Redemption period | The owner, or anyone with an interest, can pay what's owed to "redeem." For most property this period is **three years** from the sale to the state. | Minn. Stat. § 281.17(a) |
| 6. Notice of Expiration of Redemption | If the parcel hasn't been redeemed 120 days before the period ends, the county must send a Notice of Expiration of Redemption. | Minn. Stat. § 281.23, subd. 1 |
| 7. Last day to redeem | Redemption expires on the **later** of 60 days after the notice is served on everyone with an interest, or the second Monday in May. | Minn. Stat. § 281.23 (notice form) |
| 8. Forfeiture | If nothing is paid and no payment plan is in place, title forfeits to the state in trust for local taxing districts. | Minn. Stat. ch. 281–282; Hennepin County |
| 9. Public sale | The county must offer the property at public auction, generally within six months of the forfeiture certificate being filed or the property being vacated, whichever is later. | Minn. Stat. § 282.005, subd. 4 |
| 10. Surplus | If the sale brings in more than the taxes, penalties, interest and costs, former owners and other interested parties may claim the surplus. | Minn. Stat. § 282.005, subd. 6 |

### Exceptions to the three-year redemption period

- **One year** for non-homestead property located in a "targeted community" (Minn. Stat. § 281.17(b)). Some Minneapolis neighborhoods have carried that designation; check your parcel with the county.
- **Five weeks** for certain abandoned properties, but only after a city, county or housing authority gets a court order (Minn. Stat. § 281.173).
- The county uses the property's classification for the assessment year the judgment is based on, so a later change in homestead status doesn't change the redemption period (Minn. Stat. § 281.17(d)).

## The letters you might receive, and what they mean

- **Delinquent tax notice (early in the year after taxes were due).** Your taxes are on the county's delinquent list. You can still pay, or object if you believe the tax isn't owed.
- **Annual notices during the redemption period.** Hennepin sends a breakdown of what's owed each year of the redemption period.
- **Notice of Expiration of Redemption.** The most urgent letter. It lists the amount needed to redeem and the last day to do it. It also states that if the property forfeits and sells for more than what's owed, you may be entitled to the surplus (Minn. Stat. § 281.23).
- **Surplus notice and claim form (after a forfeited property sells).** Sent within 60 days of the sale if the sale produced a surplus. **You must file the claim to get paid** (Minn. Stat. § 282.005, subds. 6–7).

## Your options, in the order most families should consider them

### 1. Pay the delinquent balance (redeem)

Paying the full amount owed, at any point before redemption expires, ends the forfeiture process. Hennepin County lets you see the balance and pay through its property information search. **Contact:** Hennepin County, 612-348-3734, taxinfo@hennepin.us.

### 2. A payment plan (confession of judgment)

Minnesota lets owners pay delinquent taxes over time through a confession of judgment: a down payment, then equal annual installments. The plan runs **10 years for most property** and 5 years for commercial-industrial and public utility property, and current taxes must be kept up (DOR Red Book; Minn. Stat. § 279.37). Missing a payment can end the plan. Ask Hennepin County whether your property qualifies.

### 3. Programs for seniors and homesteads

Homeowners 65 or older may qualify for the Senior Citizens' Property Tax Deferral Program (Minn. Stat. ch. 290B). Homesteads may qualify for a property tax refund (ch. 290A), though not while taxes are delinquent (DOR Red Book). Hennepin County also points residents to local housing liaisons.

### 4. Sell the property before forfeiture

If keeping the property isn't realistic, selling before redemption expires lets the delinquent taxes be paid from the sale at closing, with the rest going to the owners. That is usually far better than forfeiture, where the property goes to auction and the family must claim any surplus afterward.

ARCH Relief is one possible buyer. You can also list with a real estate agent or sell to someone you know. Whoever you sell to, **get every term in writing and have it reviewed** before you sign.

### 5. After forfeiture: repurchase or claim the surplus

- **Repurchase.** Before the county's public sale, an interested party may repurchase the property by paying all delinquent taxes and assessments, plus penalties, interest and costs (Minn. Stat. § 282.005, subd. 3). Hennepin County says repurchase is available until the property is first offered for sale.
- **Surplus.** If the sale produces a surplus, you have **six months from the date the first notice is mailed** to file a claim (Minn. Stat. § 282.005, subd. 6(a)). A second notice goes out 90–120 days after the sale if no claim has been filed (subd. 7). If no one claims it in time, the right to the surplus ends (subd. 9).
- **Forfeitures before 2024.** Hennepin County properties forfeited and sold between August 2012 and December 2023 were part of a class-action settlement after *Tyler v. Hennepin County*. Claim deadlines may have passed; check mntaxforfeituresettlement.com or contact legal aid.

ARCH Relief doesn't arrange payment plans, redemptions, repurchases or surplus claims. Those go directly through Hennepin County, and legal aid or a Minnesota attorney can help.

## How to judge any offer to buy a tax-delinquent home

Minnesota has specific consumer protections for homeowners facing tax forfeiture (Minn. Stat. ch. 325N). Whoever you talk to, watch for these:

- **Every term should be in writing** before you sign a deed or any document that transfers the property.
- **Be cautious of anyone who asks you to sign a deed first** and promises paperwork later.
- **Be cautious of promises that you can stay in the home or buy it back later.** Arrangements like that are tightly regulated in Minnesota.
- **Anyone offering to "save" your home for a fee** is regulated as a foreclosure consultant and can't take money before finishing the work.
- **Compare the offer with what you'd have after selling on the open market**, minus the taxes owed.
- **You can always have your own attorney review an offer.** A legitimate buyer will welcome it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have before my house forfeits?**
For most property, about three years after the state bids it in at the May judgment sale, plus the notice period at the end (Minn. Stat. §§ 280.01, 281.17, 281.23). Your Notice of Expiration of Redemption gives the exact last day.

**Can the county take my house the first year I miss a payment?**
No. Taxes first become delinquent, then go to judgment, then the redemption period runs. Forfeiture comes years after the first missed payment, and paying at any point before redemption expires stops it.

**The owner died. Can the family still redeem?**
Yes. Anyone with an interest in the property may redeem during the redemption period, and after forfeiture the owner's heirs or representatives are among those who may repurchase (Minn. Stat. § 282.241, subd. 1). See our guide to [inherited property](/inherited-property/).

**If the house forfeits, do I lose everything?**
Not necessarily. If the county's sale brings in more than what was owed, former owners can claim the surplus, but only by filing a claim within six months of the first notice (Minn. Stat. § 282.005, subd. 6).
