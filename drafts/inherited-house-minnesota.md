# Selling an inherited house in Minnesota, step by step

When someone dies owning a house in Minnesota, the house doesn't automatically become sellable by the family. Someone has to get legal authority to sell it, the people who inherit it have to be identified, and any taxes or liens get settled. This guide explains how that usually works, what changes after three years, and how families sell.

*This is general information, not legal advice. Probate decisions should be made with a licensed Minnesota attorney.*

## Step 1: Find out how the house was owned

How the deed was written decides almost everything:

- **Owned with someone else as joint tenants.** The surviving joint tenant usually already owns the house. A recorded death certificate and affidavit of survivorship typically clears the record. No probate may be needed.
- **Held in a trust, or with a transfer-on-death deed.** The house passes outside probate to the named person.
- **Owned by the person alone, or with others as tenants in common.** That person's interest usually has to pass through a court process: probate, or later a decree of descent.

You can see how a Hennepin County property is titled through the county's property records, or a title company can pull the deed history.

## Step 2: Find out who inherits

- **If there's a will**, it names who inherits, subject to court approval.
- **If there's no will**, Minnesota law sets the order: the surviving spouse, then children and their descendants, then parents, then brothers and sisters and their descendants, and so on (Minn. Stat. §§ 524.2-102, 524.2-103).
- **The family home has its own rule.** If there's a surviving spouse **and** children, the spouse gets the right to live in the homestead for life, and the children share what's called the remainder (Minn. Stat. § 524.2-402(a)). That's different from each person owning a slice of the house, and it matters a great deal when selling.

## Step 3: Choose the right court process

| Situation | Usual path | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Less than 3 years since death | **Informal or formal probate** (Minn. Stat. §§ 524.3-301, 524.3-401) | The court appoints a personal representative, who can sell estate real estate (Minn. Stat. § 524.3-715(23)). |
| More than 3 years since death, no probate ever opened | **Decree of descent** (Minn. Stat. §§ 525.31, 525.312) | The court determines who inherited the property; then the heirs sign the sale. |
| A surviving spouse has a homestead interest | Either path, plus the spouse's **written consent** to any sale by the personal representative (Minn. Stat. § 524.3-715(23)) | Protects the spouse's right to the home. |

**The three-year rule.** Generally, probate or an appointment proceeding can't be started more than three years after death (Minn. Stat. § 524.3-108(a)). After that, a decree of descent is usually the path.

## Step 4: Watch for claims against the house

- **Property taxes keep running.** A death doesn't pause tax forfeiture. If taxes are behind, see our guide to [tax forfeiture in Hennepin County](/tax-forfeiture/).
- **Medical Assistance.** If the owner received Medical Assistance, the state may have a claim against the estate, including the home (Minn. Stat. §§ 256B.15, 524.2-402(c)). A probate attorney will check this.
- **Mortgages, liens and judgments** are usually paid from the sale at closing.

## Step 5: Sell

Once someone has authority to sell, either a personal representative or the heirs after a decree of descent, the sale works much like any other:

1. Agree on the price and terms in writing.
2. A title company confirms who has to sign and what has to be paid.
3. At closing, taxes, liens and costs are paid, and the rest goes to the estate or the heirs.

**If not every heir wants to sell,** an heir can often sell just their own share. See [selling your share](/sell-your-share/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do we have to open probate before we can sell?**
Usually some court process is needed if the house was in the deceased person's name alone. Which one depends on timing: probate within three years, or a decree of descent after (Minn. Stat. §§ 524.3-108, 525.31).

**Mom died, Dad is still living, and we're the kids. Who owns the house?**
If it was their homestead and there was no will, Dad generally holds the right to live there for life, and the children share the remainder (Minn. Stat. § 524.2-402(a)). Selling usually needs everyone, including Dad. Talk with a probate attorney.

**It's been more than three years. Is it too late?**
No. A decree of descent is the usual route after three years (Minn. Stat. §§ 525.31, 525.312).

**One of the heirs can't be found. Now what?**
That's common. The court process has ways to deal with missing heirs, and we research public records to help locate them.
